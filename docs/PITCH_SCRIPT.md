# AIRSHIFT — optional spoken pitch (about 90 seconds, ~230 words)

What if you could see the air you breathe?

The World Health Organization says 99% of people worldwide breathe air above its guideline limits. But that's a global, outdoor number. It doesn't tell a student what's in the air of their own classroom. Most schools have no affordable way to find out, choose a fix, and check that it worked.

I'm May Ahmed Mourad, a Grade 12 student at ADNOC Schools Madinat Zayed in Al Dhafra. AIRSHIFT is my student-led answer, in four steps. **Detect:** measure fine-particle pollution with affordable sensors. **Decide:** compare portable HEPA filtration, source control and a facilities review, with real costs. **Verify:** measure again under matched conditions and report what changed, with the limits. **Replicate:** publish the method so the next school doesn't start from zero.

I've built a working prototype to show how it would run: a 3D air lab, a room map, an intervention planner and an evidence dashboard. Everything in it is clearly labeled simulated. I'm not claiming a problem at my school. The pilot starts by measuring, and it only acts if the data says so.

With $1,500 I can run a complete eight-week pilot: two monitors, portable HEPA filtration, filters, and a published toolkit. At $1,000 or $500 I deliver a smaller, equally honest version.

You can't fix what you can't see. AIRSHIFT makes the air visible, tests the fix, and shares it. Thank you.

---

Delivery notes: pause after the first question; slow down on "Detect, Decide, Verify, Replicate"; do not add health claims if asked. Suggested answers to likely questions:

- *Is the air at your school bad?* "I don't know, and that's the point. No one has measured it. The baseline comes first."
- *Are the numbers in the prototype real?* "No. They're synthetic and labeled. They show how the dashboards behave."
- *Do HEPA filters work?* "Portable HEPA cleaners remove particles from the air they process; EPA guidance says they supplement source control and ventilation. Whether one helps in a specific room is exactly what the verify step measures."
- *What if the result is inconclusive?* "I publish it with the caveats. That still helps the next school."
