## Problem statement (207 words)

Air pollution is one of the world's most significant environmental health challenges. The World Health Organization reports that almost all of the global population (99%) breathe air that exceeds its guideline limits, and associates ambient and household air pollution with about 7 million premature deaths each year. Fine particles known as PM2.5, smaller than 2.5 micrometres, are a leading concern. But these are global, outdoor statistics. They do not tell a student what is in the air of their own classroom, where they spend much of the day. Most schools have no affordable way to measure indoor air, interpret the readings, choose a fix within budget, or check that it worked.

AIRSHIFT begins in Al Dhafra, in the UAE's desert interior. Published monitoring work describes wind-blown desert dust as a major particulate source across Abu Dhabi emirate, but that is an outdoor, regional picture. Whether classroom air at ADNOC Schools Madinat Zayed is affected is simply unknown, because no one has measured it. Indoor particles can come from outdoor dust entering through doors and gaps, foot traffic, chalk, cleaning products and ageing filters. Students, teachers and staff share that air. Our proposed pilot, subject to school approval, starts with a measured baseline instead of assuming a problem.

*To trim to exactly 200 words, delete the sentence "Students, teachers and staff share that air." (7 words). The deck keeps it because it names who is affected.*

## Mission statement (45 words)

To empower schools to understand their air quality, implement affordable evidence-based improvements, and measure their impact. Beginning with a student-led pilot in Al Dhafra, AIRSHIFT aims to create an accessible, replicable model that enables young changemakers worldwide to turn environmental awareness into measurable community action.
